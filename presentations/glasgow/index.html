01When & why it was founded

0000

A university for Scotland

The University of Glasgow was founded in 1451 by a papal bull of Pope Nicholas V, at the request of King James II and Bishop William Turnbull. Scotland wanted its own centre of learning, so that young Scots would no longer have to travel abroad to study. Today it is the second-oldest university in Scotland and the fourth-oldest in the English-speaking world.

02The first disciplines

What students learned

In its early years the university followed the model of the University of Bologna. Teaching took place in and around Glasgow Cathedral, and the papal bull allowed faculties of Theology, Canon and Civil Law and Arts. Most students began with the Arts course, which prepared them for careers in the Church, the law and public service.

03Why it is famous

Cradle of the Enlightenment

Glasgow was a centre of the Scottish Enlightenment. Here James Watt, the university's instrument maker, improved the steam engine, Joseph Lister developed antiseptic surgery, and Lord Kelvin transformed physics. Its neo-Gothic main building on Gilmorehill, with its famous cloisters, is one of the most beautiful in Britain. Today Glasgow is a member of the Russell Group and one of the world's top universities.
